# Beginner Guide: Caerleon Faction Warfare

We flag for Caerleon. It plays nothing like the other five factions, so don't go in expecting a zerg army to join.

## How To Flag

Head to Caerleon, click the flag button, and hold still for 15 seconds while it charges. That's it, you're flagged. You'll need to travel back to Caerleon any time you want to re-flag, you can only flag for whatever city you're standing in.

## What Happens If You Lose a Fight

This depends entirely on the zone you're in, not the flag itself:

- **Blue and Yellow zones:** you get knocked down, no gear lost. Low stakes, good for learning.
- **Red zones:** full loot. Everything you had equipped plus your inventory becomes lootable.
- **Black zones:** always full loot no matter what, that's baseline black zone risk, unrelated to being flagged.

Red zone kills also pay about triple the faction points of a yellow zone kill, so there's a real incentive to push deeper once you're ready.

## How Outposts Actually Work

Outposts are guarded by at least 7 Guards plus a Champion. Clear those, and a capture ring appears, stay inside it until the bar fills to take it. You need at least 3 players for the boss fight, mechanics get significantly harder below that number. There's a tradeoff built into group size: more players means a safer fight and better odds of defending against another faction jumping in, but the reward splits thinner per person. Fewer players means a bigger individual cut, but more risk. A captured outpost locks out other factions from contesting it again for about 20 minutes.

To actually receive a reward share, you need at least 700 average IP equipped, gear up before joining a push, showing up under-geared means fighting for nothing.

## How Caerleon Actually Plays

There's no big faction army here. Other Caerleon-flagged players can attack each other too, everyone's out for themselves. Captured Outposts and regions don't generate ongoing value the way they do for the other five factions, for Caerleon they turn into bandit camps instead, no shared defensive value, but real, direct profit for whoever's holding them. That profit isn't shared with the wider Caerleon faction, it goes straight to the capturing group. The real play is small squads (think 3-6 people, matching the outpost boss requirement) raiding Outposts, or third-partying other factions' fights mid-battle and cleaning up.

Because there's no army to fall back on, faction pushes only work if they're actually organized. Solo wandering into it works for learning in blue/yellow, but real pushes need a group and a plan.

## Getting Started Checklist

1. Flag up and get a feel for it in blue/yellow zones first, no gear risk.
2. Get to at least 700 average IP before joining any push aiming at an outpost, otherwise there's no reward share waiting for you.
3. Join a scheduled faction push (Monday-Friday, 23:00-12:00 UTC) once you're ready for red zone stakes.
4. Move in a small, coordinated squad of 3-6, not solo, once you're pushing Outposts or third-partying fights.
